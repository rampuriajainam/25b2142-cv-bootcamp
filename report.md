# CIFAR-10N (worst) — report · Jainam Rampuria (25b2142)

Figures and the longer write-up: `code/ANALYSIS.md`. Clean labels were used only to measure, never to train or choose.

**Q1.** 40.2% of training labels are wrong, and the errors are structured: the 8 most common (true→given) pairs hold 33% of all errors, versus 10% for the uniform synthetic labels. Truck↔automobile alone is 13% (1,247 + 1,126), cat↔dog 10% (924 + 834), then deer→horse (683), deer→dog (491), airplane→ship (379) and frog→bird (373). These are look-alike classes: vehicles, four-legged animals, sky/water backgrounds. Cat and deer are the noisiest classes (51% wrong), horse the cleanest (28%).

**Q2.** Epoch 37. Up to epoch 36 the network fits only 12–16% of mislabeled images to their wrong label while accuracy on correctly labeled images rises to 0.90: it learns the real pattern and overrules the noise. From epoch 37, as the cosine learning rate shrinks, the mislabeled fit climbs steadily (0.19 → 0.68 at epoch 60) and validation accuracy falls (noisy 0.56 → 0.50, clean 0.79 → 0.69). Early stopping on noisy validation picked epoch 35, which turned out to be exactly the best clean-validation epoch (0.794).

**Q3.** Submitted (fixed in advance): 5-fold out-of-fold confidence, 1 − p(given label) from the fold model that never trained on the image, averaged over all 60 epochs. AUROC 0.945, precision@k 0.838. Averaging matters: final epoch alone gives 0.875, because late epochs memorize. My clean-label check found in-sample epoch-averaged loss slightly better (0.960); I did not switch, since that would select using clean labels. Failure mode: plausible confusions. Only 46% of truck→automobile and 57% of cat→dog errors are caught, versus 94% of other errors. The models genuinely agree with annotators on ambiguous images.

**Q4.** DivideMix was best on noisy validation (0.593, versus GCE 0.572, filter 0.572, early-stopped CE 0.558). Each of two networks fits a 2-component Gaussian mixture to per-image loss; low-loss images keep their labels and the rest become unlabeled. Each network trains on its peer's split, so it cannot confirm its own mistakes, and label refinement, co-guessing and MixUp extract signal without trusting the labels. The split was 93% pure and set aside 90% of wrong labels. Only 10% of wrong labels were fitted (CE: 68%), giving 92.0% clean validation accuracy versus 94.7% for the clean-label reference.

**Q5.** No. Both noise types give DivideMix > GCE > cross-entropy (clean validation, selected epochs: 92.0 / 82.3 / 79.4 human versus 94.2 / 87.7 / 82.5 synthetic). But every method does better on synthetic noise at the same 40% rate. Uniform errors contradict the image, so they separate cleanly: DivideMix's split was 98.7% pure and caught 98% of wrong labels, versus 93% and 90% on human noise. Before memorizing (epochs 5–35), cross-entropy agreed with 20–30% of the top human confusions but only 4–6% of synthetic errors. Human noise is partly learnable, so it is harder to remove.
